Richard Gabriel's influential essay contrasting the MIT approach (correctness
and completeness first) with the New Jersey approach (implementation simplicity
first) to software design. Originally a section of "Lisp: Good News, Bad News,
How to Win Big." Argues that simpler, less correct software spreads virally
and outcompetes more correct alternatives through portability and adoption.
