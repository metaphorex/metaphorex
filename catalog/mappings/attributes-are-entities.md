## What It Brings

We treat abstract qualities as if they were things. Courage, beauty,
intelligence, patience -- none of these exist as discrete objects in the
world, yet we talk about them as if you could pick them up, hand them
over, or put them in a drawer. ATTRIBUTES ARE ENTITIES is an ontological
metaphor that reifies qualities into objects, making them available for
the full range of operations we perform on physical things: possessing,
transferring, measuring, losing, finding.

Key structural parallels:

- **Attributes as possessions** -- "She has great courage." "He lacks
  patience." To have an attribute is to possess an entity. This turns
  qualities into things that can be gained or lost, given or taken away.
  The grammar follows naturally: attributes fill the object slot in
  transitive constructions.
- **Attributes as substances with quantity** -- "She has a lot of
  determination." "There wasn't an ounce of decency in him." Attributes
  are not just objects but measurable stuff. The metaphor imports the
  logic of quantity: more determination is better, you can run out of
  patience, and courage can be depleted.
- **Attributes as transferable objects** -- "She gave him confidence."
  "The experience robbed him of his innocence." Attributes can change
  hands, which allows us to describe the causal effects of experience
  on character. Events become transactions that add or remove attributes
  from a person's inventory.
- **Attributes as independent existents** -- "Beauty exists in every
  culture." "Justice demands action." The most abstract version: the
  attribute is not merely possessed by someone but exists on its own,
  as an entity in the world. This maps onto Platonic realism -- the
  philosophical position that abstract qualities have independent
  existence -- but operates in everyday language without requiring
  philosophical commitment.

## Where It Breaks

- **Attributes are not discrete** -- the metaphor treats qualities as
  bounded objects, but most attributes are continuous and contextual.
  "Intelligence" is not a single thing you either have or lack; it
  manifests differently in different situations. The entity metaphor
  encourages essentialist thinking: "He is brave" treats courage as a
  stable possession rather than a context-dependent pattern of behavior.
- **The transfer model is misleading** -- "She gave him confidence"
  suggests a zero-sum transaction, but the giver's confidence is not
  diminished. The physical-object logic of transfer (one person loses
  what another gains) does not apply to attributes, yet the metaphor
  imports it anyway.
- **Reification invites false precision** -- once attributes are entities,
  they become countable and measurable. IQ tests, personality inventories,
  and performance metrics all depend on this reification. The metaphor
  makes it feel natural to assign a number to "intelligence" or
  "leadership ability," but the precision is an artifact of the
  metaphor, not a property of the attribute.
- **The metaphor obscures relational qualities** -- "fairness" is not
  a thing someone possesses but a property of interactions between
  people. By treating it as an entity, the metaphor locates fairness
  inside an individual rather than in the structure of a situation.

## Expressions

- "She has great patience" -- a quality as a possessed entity
- "He lost his nerve" -- an attribute departing like a lost object
- "That took a lot of courage" -- an event consuming a quantity of
  an attribute-substance
- "She found her voice" -- an attribute as something misplaced and
  recovered
- "He's full of energy" -- the body as a container holding an
  attribute-substance
- "The job robbed him of his dignity" -- a causal event as theft
  of an attribute-object
- "There's not an ounce of honesty in him" -- an attribute measured
  in physical units
- "Beauty is in the eye of the beholder" -- a partial correction,
  relocating the entity from the object to the perceiver
- "She radiates confidence" -- an attribute as a substance emitted
  by its possessor

## Origin Story

ATTRIBUTES ARE ENTITIES is documented in the Master Metaphor List (Lakoff,
Espenson & Schwartz, 1991) and is part of the broader ontological metaphor
system described in *Metaphors We Live By* (1980). Lakoff and Johnson
identify ontological metaphors as a fundamental class: they take
experiences and abstract concepts that have no clear boundaries and impose
entity structure on them, allowing us to refer to them, quantify them,
identify aspects of them, and reason about them. This metaphor is closely
related to PROPERTIES ARE POSSESSIONS (the object case of the Event
Structure metaphor system) but is more general -- it covers not just
possessions but any reification of an abstract quality into a thing-like
entity.
