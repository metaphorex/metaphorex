The deliberate shaping of animal behavior through systematic reward and
punishment. Distinguished from animal husbandry (herd management, breeding,
pasture) by its focus on behavioral compliance in individual animals.
Distinguished from animal behavior (wild instinct, predation, territory) by
the explicit presence of a human trainer who designs the conditioning regime.
The trainer-trainee relationship is asymmetric: the trainer controls the
reward signal, sets the criteria for success, and decides when the animal has
learned. The animal has no access to the trainer's reasons -- it learns to
associate actions with outcomes without understanding the goal. As a source
domain, animal training maps onto any system where behavior is shaped through
feedback without shared understanding of purpose.
