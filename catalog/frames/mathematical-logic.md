The formal study of valid inference, provability, and the limits of formal
systems. As a source domain, mathematical logic supplies impossibility
theorems (Goedel, Arrow, Rice), completeness results, and the distinction
between syntax and semantics. Its metaphorical power derives from showing
that certain desirable properties cannot coexist -- that limits are inherent
in the structure of the system, not failures of implementation. When we say
a problem is "undecidable" or a set of requirements is "impossible to
satisfy simultaneously," we are borrowing from this frame.
