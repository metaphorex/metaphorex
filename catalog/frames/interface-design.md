The design of boundaries between components, systems, or users and systems.
As a target domain, interface design borrows structure from physical joints
(tongue-and-groove, dovetail), diplomatic protocol (handshake, negotiation),
and surface contact (friction, seamlessness). The domain foregrounds the
tension between constraint and flexibility: an interface must be strict enough
to ensure compatibility and loose enough to accommodate variation.
