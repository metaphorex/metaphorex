Venturing into unknown territory to discover what lies beyond the current
boundary of knowledge or habitation. The exploration frame provides a spatial
metaphor for epistemic progress: the known is mapped territory, the unknown is
uncharted wilderness, and the explorer is anyone who pushes past the current
edge. Structurally productive because it supplies direction (outward), risk
(the unknown is dangerous), reward (discovery), and a persistent boundary
(the frontier) that recedes as knowledge advances.
