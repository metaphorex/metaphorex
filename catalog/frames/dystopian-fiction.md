Fictional narratives depicting societies under oppressive, dehumanizing
governance -- typically extrapolating present-day political or technological
trends to their worst-case endpoints. Dystopian fiction is structurally
productive as a source domain because it names the mechanisms of control
(surveillance, pleasure, language manipulation, enforced conformity) with a
clarity that real political discourse rarely achieves. The genre's concepts
have escaped their source texts to become standard frames for reasoning
about real governance, technology policy, and civil liberties. The key
structural feature is the totalizing system: dystopias present control as
comprehensive, inescapable, and often invisible to those within it.
