The process of identifying a disease or condition from its signs and symptoms,
guided by probabilistic reasoning about what is common versus what is rare.
As a source domain, clinical diagnosis provides a decision framework for any
situation where an observer must interpret ambiguous signals: start with the
most likely explanation, rule it out before considering exotic alternatives,
and remember that the prior probability of a condition matters more than the
vividness of its presentation. The frame encodes the tension between
thoroughness (never miss a rare disease) and efficiency (most hoofbeats are
horses).
