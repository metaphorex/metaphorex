The mathematical study of collections and membership. As a source domain,
set theory supplies the vocabulary of classification itself: what belongs,
what is excluded, what overlaps. Its most productive metaphorical exports
are its pathologies -- the paradoxes that arise when classification is
turned on itself. Russell's paradox, the barber who shaves all those who
don't shave themselves, and Cantor's diagonal argument all reveal that
naive classification breaks under self-reference. When we ask "does this
category include itself?" in governance, taxonomy, or software type systems,
we are borrowing from set theory's hard-won understanding of what happens
when containment becomes recursive.
