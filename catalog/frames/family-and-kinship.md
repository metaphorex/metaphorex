The structure of biological and social kinship relationships, with their
asymmetries of age, care, authority, and developmental stage. As a source
domain, family foregrounds the distinction between the caretaker and the
dependent, the persistence of early relational patterns into adulthood, and
the idea that one entity can contain or carry another at a different
developmental stage. Structurally productive because it encodes the temporal
layering of self (the adult contains the child they once were), the
obligation of the stronger to protect the weaker, and the way early
relational templates shape later behavior.
