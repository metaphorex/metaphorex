Speculative narratives that extrapolate scientific or technological premises
to explore their consequences for individuals and societies. As a source
domain for metaphor, science fiction is distinctive because its concepts are
designed to make the unfamiliar thinkable -- and once a concept enters common
usage ("cyberspace," "the matrix," "metaverse"), it becomes the default frame
through which non-specialists reason about the corresponding real technology.
The structural feature that makes SF metaphors powerful and dangerous in equal
measure is the genre's tendency to personalize systemic phenomena: giving a
face (Skynet, HAL, the Borg) to what is in reality a diffuse, emergent process.
