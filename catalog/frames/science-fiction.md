Speculative narratives that extrapolate scientific and technological
possibilities into imagined futures, alien encounters, and alternate
societies. As a source domain, science fiction is unusually productive
because its concepts are designed to be structurally rich -- they come
pre-loaded with internal logic, dramatic stakes, and thought-experiment
clarity. When SF concepts escape their source material to frame real-world
technology and society ("that's basically Skynet," "we're living in a
simulation"), they carry narrative assumptions -- individual heroism,
sudden singularity, binary outcomes -- that can distort understanding
of gradual, systemic realities.
